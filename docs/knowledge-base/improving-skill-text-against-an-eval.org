* Where the agent shortens a citation
Across every wording tried, a citation lost its title in the same few
sentences:
- describing code: "`src/loans.py` implements `F-1 §1` and `F-1 §2` only";
- mapping sections to a successor's: "Its status maps `F-1 §1` and `F-1 §3` to carried-over sections";
- naming the record as a thing, late in the reply: "`F-1` gets a successor", "`F-2` and `ADR-1` stay as they are";
- quoting a requirement whose text carries a citation, shortening the citation inside the quote.
The agent gives the first citation of each record whole. A rule that says
"every time" lowers the count without removing it. Examples shaped like the
failing sentence lowered it further than prohibitions did.

* What the sources say about improving text against an eval
- A keep-if-better loop: one change at a time, kept when the score improves
  and reset otherwise, with a log of each attempt. Karpathy's autoresearch
  (https://github.com/karpathy/autoresearch, its `program.md`) runs one on a
  single score with no repeats. It cites no paper.
- The proposer does better when it sees why a run failed, not only its
  score. GEPA (https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19457) gives its reflecting model
  the failing output and keeps a candidate only if it beats its parent on a
  small batch first.
- Selecting on the questions the wording was tuned against overfits.
  Optimisers hold a set out and pick the final wording on it: OPRO
  (https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03409), Anthropic's `skill-creator`
  (`scripts/run_loop.py`, a 60/40 split, chosen by test score).
- A difference between two wordings counts when it exceeds the noise:
  repeat each question, and compare the means against their standard errors
  (https://www.anthropic.com/research/statistical-approach-to-model-evals).
- For a rule about the shape of output, a template shapes it better than a
  prohibition (obra/superpowers, `writing-skills`).
No tool found keeps the edited text readable or in a house style. That is
left to the proposer's instructions and to the record keeper's review.

* The loop in use from 2026-10-26
- Questions :: six on the library-loans fixture,
  `evals/cases/citing-records-to-the-record-keeper/prompts/`. Four train
  (renewing a reserved book, auditing the code, mapping a successor, framing
  a complaint). Two are held out (counting renewals, releasing a held copy);
  the proposer never sees them.
- Score :: `npm run score:citations` (`evals/scripts/score-citations.mjs`).
  Each reply's score is the sum of its faults under the four F-20261024-5tt7 checks
  divided by its citations; a wording's score is the mean over replies, with
  its standard error. Lower is better.
- Each attempt :: the proposer (a Fable subagent) gets the house rules, the
  current wording, the failing citations from the training questions, and
  the log of attempts. It makes one change across the skills, the seed and
  both AGENTS.md copies, and commits it. The commit is scored on the training
  questions, three runs each, and compared with the current best question by
  question (`--against`): the gap is the mean over questions of the
  difference in their means, and the margin twice its standard error. It is
  kept if the gap exceeds the margin. Where the gap is positive and within
  the margin, both wordings run each question three times more and are judged
  on the pooled replies (`--pool`). Otherwise it is reverted.
- Stop :: after three discards in a row, or ten attempts.
- Final check :: the winner and the original wording, six runs of each held-out
  question. Then the record keeper reviews the diff.
- Only the with-skills arm runs: no skill loaded in any run of this case, so
  the without-skills arm repeats the with-skills arm at twice the cost.

* Comparing two wordings question by question
The first rule compared the two wordings' means over all their replies,
against twice their combined standard error. On attempt 2, with 24 replies
each, it gave a gap of 0.1257 against a margin of 0.1259, while attempt 2
scored better on every question (0.036 against 0.085, 0.33 against 0.61,
0.054 against 0.19, 0 against 0.04). Pooling over questions counts the
spread between questions as noise: auditing the code always scores far
above the other three. Compared question by question, as
https://www.anthropic.com/research/statistical-approach-to-model-evals
recommends for paired items, the margin was 0.067. The record keeper chose
this comparison on 2026-10-26, from attempt 2 on. It was chosen after it
would change attempt 2's verdict; the held-out check guards the result.

* What the proposer can read
The held-out questions are in the repository, so the proposer can read

attempt 3 the proposer read the scorer's raw results under
`/tmp/score-citations-*`, and one of them held the original wording's
replies to the held-out questions. That file was moved out of `/tmp` before
attempt 3 was scored. From then on the held-out questions are run only at
the final check.
