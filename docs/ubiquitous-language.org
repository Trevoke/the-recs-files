#+TITLE: Ubiquitous language

* Record management
The bounded context of record changes and of sweeping words through a
project.
** Record keeper
The person or team who directs a project's record changes.
** Agent
The model that the recs files instruct, which drafts and writes record
changes at the record keeper's direction.
** Record
A short, numbered statement of one truth about a system, with a title that
states its substance. Every record belongs to one category.
** Category
The kind a record is. It gives the record its form and the prefix of its
number.
** Projection
The listing of every standing or proposed record in one category, in
number order, named after the category.
** Standing record
An accepted record that is not withdrawn.
** Record change
Creating, superseding, retiring or clarifying a record.
** Citation
A reference to a record by its number and title, or to a heading of the
knowledge base.
** Term
A word, or a few words, that the ubiquitous language defines within one
bounded context.
** Falsified sentence
A sentence that a record change makes untrue.
** Sweep
One pass through a project that carries an agreed change to a term, or to
a record's citations, into every sentence it reaches.
** Clarification
An in-place edit to a record's wording that keeps its intent, as the record
keeper decides.
** Proposed
The status of a record written for the record keeper's agreement, before
they have given it.
** Accepted
The status of a record the record keeper has agreed to.
** Rejected
The status of a proposed record the record keeper declined. Its text
stands as written and its number stays taken.
** Withdrawn
An accepted record that no longer holds.
** Superseded
The status of a withdrawn record that one or more successors replace. Its
text stands as written.
** Successor
A record that replaces one or more superseded records.
** Retired
The status of a withdrawn record that nothing replaces. Its text stands as
written.
** Placement tests
The two questions that settle a record's category: whether describing it
names a verb, an argument or a sequence of events, and whether its word
would survive a change of implementation.
