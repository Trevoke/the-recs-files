* ADR-20261015-dytn Each term is a record of its own
:PROPERTIES:
:STATUS: Accepted
:END:
** Context
The ubiquitous language was kept as one file of terms without identifiers. A
redefinition changes what a term means, and every sentence that uses the
term reads differently after it.
[[./adr-20261015-nz3v-a-changed-decision-is-a-new-record-that-supersedes-the-old.org][ADR-20261015-nz3v: A changed decision is a new record that supersedes the old]]
makes every change to what a record decides a new record; a term without an
identifier cannot be superseded, so redefining one needed an exception to
[[./adr-20261015-nz3v-a-changed-decision-is-a-new-record-that-supersedes-the-old.org][ADR-20261015-nz3v: A changed decision is a new record that supersedes the old]].
** Decision
We will keep each term, and each bounded context, as a ubiquitous language
record with a UL identifier, in a file of its own and titled with the term. The
projection lists the terms grouped under their bounded contexts.
** Consequences
A redefinition supersedes the term's record like any other record, with
no exception. A term is cited by its record name, as
[[../ubiquitous-language/ul-20261015-5dhq-record-keeper.org][UL-20261015-5dhq: Record keeper]].
A rename that keeps a term's meaning is a clarification of its title. The
ubiquitous language takes as many files as it has terms.
